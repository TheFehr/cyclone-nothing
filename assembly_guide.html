02Build sequence

Two sub-assemblies come together independently, then join at the very end. Steps 1–3 build the clamp; step 4 builds the pivot; steps 5–6 join them and finish.

1

Hold the flange nut in the gap

clamp_center.stl + flange_nut.stl

Set flange_nut into the open gap between clamp_center's two bosses — it rests there on its own, since it's wider than either boss's bore and can't fall through either side. This is the only point in the whole build where the nut can get in at all: once the leadscrew is in place blocking either bore, there's no way to fit it through afterward.

2

Screw the leadscrew through it

+ screw.stl · CA glue

Feed the leadscrew's plain end into one boss and keep turning it inward — it threads straight through the nut you're holding in the gap, then its bare shaft (no thread to catch on) passes on through the far boss's bore. The nut is too wide to follow it through, so it stays captured in the gap once the screw is all the way in. Run a small bead of CA glue around the nut once it's seated so it can't work itself loose.

Check: once the glue sets, spin the leadscrew by hand. It should turn smoothly with no drag or axial play before you continue.
3

Thread on the claws

+ clamp_claw_right.stl + clamp_claw_left.stl

Rotate clamp_claw_right onto the screw's +X (right-hand thread) end, and clamp_claw_left onto the −X (left-hand thread) end, threading each in from the tip toward the center. Because the threads are opposite-handed, turning the leadscrew one way draws both claws in together and centers the grip automatically.

4

Close the collar around the pin

base.stl + collar_top.stl + collar_bottom.stl · 2 filament pins

Set base's pin down and bring collar_top and collar_bottom together around it — the two halves meet along the pin's own axis. Push a ~15mm length of 1.75mm filament through each of the two tabs' aligned holes to close the clamshell, and add a small drop of CA glue at each so they can't work loose. Confirm the collar spins freely on the pin before moving on.

5

Join the two sub‑assemblies

step 4 result + step 3 result · the last 2 filament pins

Push your last 2 filament pins into the two holes at collar_bottom's standoff rod tip, leaving them protruding slightly. Bring the clamp sub‑assembly (from step 3) up to meet the rod, aligning the connect shelf on clamp_center so the protruding pin ends slide into its matching holes as the two faces come together — like a standard dowel joint. Add a small drop of CA glue at each pin once seated. This is the joint that makes the rail and claws actually rotate with the collar — everything downstream of it now turns together as one unit.

6

Glue on the knob

+ knob.stl

Put a small drop of CA or epoxy on the screw's square peg and press knob on until it seats flush. The square peg keeps it from spinning loose under turning torque. Give the glue its full cure time before using the knob to adjust the clamp.

Done: the phone's short edges seat in the two claws, centered on the true pivot axis in both length and width. Turn the knob to open or close the grip; rotate the whole clamp 90° on the collar to swap portrait and landscape.
